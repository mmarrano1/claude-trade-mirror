Guide

What this page is

This is a read-only copy of one person's Claude Finance app. The app runs on its owner's Mac and keeps an eye on the owner's Schwab account. This site shows the same numbers on a phone or iPad. Every copy of Claude Finance has its own site and its own code: yours shows your account and nobody else's. After you unlock it, More says whose copy you're looking at.

It can't do anything. No buttons place trades, move money or change settings. It only shows what the owner's Mac already knows.

It isn't advice. It shows what the owner's own rules and Firm say and why. The decisions are the owner's.

How to open it

  1. On the Mac, open Claude Finance → Settings → Phone. It shows this site's address. Open it in Safari on the phone.
  2. Type the code (Settings → Phone → Show the code on the Mac). Capitals, spaces and dashes don't matter.
  3. Leave “Remember on this device” on, and you won't need the code again on this phone.
  4. Best: tap Share → Add to Home Screen. It then opens full screen like an app, the bottom tabs work with one tap (in Safari the first tap only brings back Safari's bar), and it opens even without a connection. You'll type the code once more there.

Is it private?

Yes. Before anything leaves the Mac, the numbers are locked with the code. The website only stores the locked copy, which is just scrambled text without the code. Only someone with the code can read it, so only give the code to someone you want to see the whole account. To lock everyone out, change it on the Mac (Settings → Phone → Change the code): the old code stops working at once, on every phone.

What you're looking at

The tabs at the bottom.

Positions
Your account is the owner's whole Schwab account (stocks plus cash). The chart compares it with putting the same money in the S&P 500 (the dashed line). Money moved in from the bank is never counted as a gain. Drag a finger across any chart to read a day, and pinch to zoom.
Things to do lists orders waiting on you, if there are any.
Your positions shows each stock in the account: today's price, the gain or loss since it was bought, and one line saying what happens next (for example “Falls to $24.84 → Schwab sells it by itself”). Tap one for its chart with what was paid, the bot's note and Claude's research.
On the way and Coming up show orders and bank transfers in progress, earnings reports, and when a planned holding period ends.
Check a stock
Type a ticker or a company name (NVDA, Apple, Costco…). For the roughly 1,500 stocks the Mac scans, the answer shows up right away: what the bot thinks (would buy or not now, and why), a checkmark for each of the five entry rules, and how the stock has done. Closest to a buy lists the stocks the bot likes most today.
For any other stock, or for the bot's full write-up, tap Ask the bot. That sends the ticker (locked with the code) to the Mac, which checks it and sends the answer back here, usually within a few minutes while Claude Finance is open on the Mac. Asking never buys anything.
More
Turn on notifications, open Full view (the same screens as on the Mac), look for an update, open this guide, or lock the page.
Three horizons
The account is split by how long something is meant to be held. Each has its own rules:
  • Long-termIndex funds (Nasdaq-100 and S&P 500) kept for years and never sold because of a drop.
  • Medium-termResearched company stocks held for months up to a year, with only an emergency stop (−40%).
  • Short-termAbout 2-month trades of the strongest stocks: sell at −20% (stop-loss) or +40% (take-profit), or after about 2 months.

A few words

The main ones:

Stop-loss
An order that sells automatically if the price falls to a set level, to limit a loss.
Take-profit
The price where the rules say to sell and keep the gain.
Earnings
A company's quarterly report. The stock often moves a lot the next day.
50-day / 200-day average
The stock's average price over those days. Above the 200-day line usually means a longer uptrend.

Is it up to date?

It updates on its own after every scan on the Mac (weekdays around 9:45 AM, noon and 4:15 PM, and whenever the app is open). The label at the top right says when the Mac last checked prices. Its dot shows how fresh that is: green is current, yellow means the Mac missed a scheduled check (it may be asleep or away), so numbers may be out of date. Tap it (or More → Check for an update) to look for a newer copy.
